# 60 fps battles

Requested by the user on 2026-10-20 (the second of two enhancements; [HD textures](HD_TEXTURES_PLAN.md) come first): battles at 60 pictures per second, although the game shows 30, as an optional toggle in the in-game overlay's Display entry ([launcher plan](../launcher/LAUNCHER_PLAN.md), Overlay).

**Requirement** (the user, 2026-10-20): the fight's behavior stays exactly as at 30: the same game speed, the same physics, the same results. 60 fps may only add pictures between the game's steps, never make the game step faster or differently. The check is the same CPU-vs-CPU fight at 30 and 60: equal results and battle times, equal positions and health at the same moments.

## What is known (2026-10-20)

- **Where the 30 comes from.** The fight's main loop (`0x102E80`, `0x102F30`) starts Timer 1 at the top of a frame, and before waiting for the vertical blank calls `0x28C790`, which spins until the timer has counted 400 lines (about 1.5 fields) and stops it. So a fight frame takes two fields. The menus' loops have no such wait and run at 60 ([performance plan](../ee/PERFORMANCE_PLAN.md), finding 3; the user confirmed 30 in fights on the original).
- **Nobody has patched it.** PCSX2's patch database has only widescreen patches for the Tenkaichi games (BT2's PAL release among them), none for frame rate, and requests for a BT2 60 fps code on the PCSX2 forums went unanswered. That suggests what the code also suggests: the fight advances a fixed step per frame, so removing the wait alone would run the fight at double speed.
- **The port's own budget.** A fight frame currently takes 18 to 27 ms of work in most stretches and up to 31.5 ms in the heaviest on this machine (performance plan, finding 7). 60 needs every frame under 16.7 ms. Whatever the method, the fight has to get about twice as fast first, or the toggle only gives an uneven rate.

- **The frame end** (read 2026-10-20): the fight's loop (`0x102F30`) ends each frame in `0x2711E8`, which six functions call: a flush (`0x284B40`), `0x310548`, the 400-line wait (`0x28C790`, Timer 1's count against 0x190), the vertical-blank wait (`0x3104B0`), and then it counts the game's frames (`0x3B0A84`). So the game steps once per 30 fps frame.

- **The engine steps by a fixed amount** (measured 2026-10-20, `BT2_FIGHT_UNCAPPED=<flip>`, which lifts the 400-line wait from that flip on): uncapped from flip 900 of a versus fight, flips 1,000 and 1,200 are reached at fields 2,285 and 2,485 instead of 2,385 and 2,785, and their pictures are pixel-equal to the capped run's. The fight goes through the same steps, one per field: 60 steps per second is the fight at double speed. The engine does not scale by the time passed, so a 60 at the engine level needs every per-step quantity (speeds, animation steps, frame counts of moves, hit-stun, timers, the CPU's reaction counts) halved or doubled, and would not reproduce the 30 fps fight exactly (the user's requirement). Way 4 stays the way: its delay is half a game frame (the in-between picture is shown when the newer frame could have been, the newer frame 16.7 ms later).

## Ways to get there

1. **Half steps.** Run the fight's frame twice as often with each frame advancing half as far. That needs every speed, timer and animation step of the fight to be scaled. Fighting games often have one place for this: slow motion (BT2 slows the action for some finishes) usually means a global time scale that the fight's systems read. If that scale exists and is honoured everywhere, 60 fps is: the 400-line wait skipped, the scale halved, and the step counters that are integers (frame counts) checked.
2. **Generated in-between pictures.** Leave the game at 30 and make a picture between each two from the rendered images (motion estimation, as frame generation does). It does not touch the game, but it adds a frame of latency, which matters in a fighting game, and the HUD and fast effects smear. A fallback only.
4. **In-between pictures drawn from the game's own commands (preferred since 2026-10-20).** The user's requirement (the fight's behavior exactly as at 30) rules out changing the game's step: half steps change the arithmetic of every movement and collision, so results and the CPU's decisions could differ. Drawn in-between pictures leave the game's step untouched, so the fight is identical by construction. The game sends each frame's 3D work as VIF1 lists with the camera and the bones' matrices for VU1; two successive frames of a fight have the same structure. An in-between picture is the frame's command stream sent again with each matrix halfway between its value in the frame before and in this one (the HUD and 2D drawn as in this frame). It costs one frame of delay like way 2 but has no smearing. Needs: telling the matrices apart in the VIF1 data (the programs' input layout is known from the hand-written VU1 programs), pairing them between frames (same position in the same list), and a rule for what does not pair (an object appearing or leaving: drawn as in the newer frame).
3. **Scaling each system by hand.** If there is no common time scale: find each per-frame step (movement, animation playback, effects, camera, timers, AI) and halve it. This is a large reverse-engineering effort with a long tail of things that run twice as fast.

## Order of work

1. **The command stream of a fight frame — done 2026-10-20** (`BT2_VIF1_DUMP`, versus fight, flips 1,000 to 1,003): two successive frames' VIF1 streams have the same structure, all 12,382 VIF codes in the same order (4,292 unpacks, 879 DIRECT blocks, 2,664 program starts, all at address 0). Their data is equal except in about 100 unpacks: 95 blocks of 34 quadwords and 5 of 12, and 150 of the 879 DIRECT blocks. A 34-quadword block holds GIF tags and integer data (quadwords 0 to 21), two 4 × 4 matrices (22 to 25 and 26 to 29: a rotation and a world position each, the two bones of the skinned-vertex program) and constants; between the frames the rotations turn a little and the positions move. The vertex data does not change. So an in-between picture is: the frame's VIF1 stream with each changing bone matrix halfway (rotation by quaternion, position linearly), the 12-quadword blocks (probably the camera) likewise, the DIRECT blocks as in the newer frame (or their positions halfway where they pair). Open: drawing it without disturbing the real frame's GS memory (a second renderer fed with a copy of GS memory at the frame's start, or the frame drawn into spare memory), and the PATH3 data and texture uploads of the frame.
2. **An in-between picture offline — done 2026-10-20.** `BT2_VIF1_REPLACE=<flip>:<file>` swaps a flip's VIF1 list (a copy: VU1 and the GS get it, guest memory does not) for one in which every float that differs between two recorded frames' V4-32 unpacks is set halfway (6,457 words; the k-th unpack of one frame paired with the k-th of the other, since DIRECT lengths differ). The whole 3D frame is one list per flip, shown two flips later (the list sent during flip 1,001 is the picture at 1,003). Result (`work/sixty/between.png`): a clean picture between the real flips 1,002 and 1,003, camera and both fighters' poses halfway, no broken geometry. The game is untouched: game memory equal page for page at field 2,400 with and without it, flip 1,100 pixel-equal. Plain blending of the matrices' components was enough at this motion; fast turns may need the rotations re-normalized.
3. **Live in-between pictures — built 2026-10-21** (`runtime/gs/gpu/in_between_frames.*`, the setting "60 fps battles"). Each frame's GS input is recorded in order (VIF1 transfers with the GS data their run produced, through the runtime's `vif1_running` flag and the sink's `on_input`; all other GIF data). A second paraLLEl-GS renderer on the main one's device draws, on a worker thread from the end of flip F's work, the in-between picture of frames F - 1 and F: the main renderer keeps its GPU state (GS memory with super-samples, and the CLUT) after each flip in one of two slots (`GpuGsBackend::keepState`), the second renderer takes the state from flip F - 1 (`takeState`, cached textures dropped) and runs frame F's input with its VIF1 lists blended halfway on a copy of VIF1/VU1 at frame F's start; a frame without an in-between picture is replayed instead. The probe shows the picture one field after flip F (the frame recorded up to flip F appears at flip F + 1, checked pixel for pixel), so no delay is added. Lists are aligned unpack by unpack (Myers' diff on the codes), because a few pieces of geometry change or come and go each frame; unaligned unpacks are drawn from the newer frame. paraLLEl-GS patches: `parallel_gs_frame_boundaries.patch` (the second renderer's flushes leave the device's frame to the main one, so it can flush from its thread) and `parallel_gs_vram_backup.patch` (the GS memory and CLUT buffers, and invalidating all pages). Checked: `BT2_SIXTY_FPS=check` draws the "in-between" all the way to the newer frame, pixel-equal to the main picture (three moments of a versus fight); in the window a fight holds 30 flips and 60 pictures per second (1,474 of 1,499 fight frames with an in-between picture, the game's thread waiting 28 to 36 ms in all for the worker), plain and with 16x, HD textures and widescreen (work per flip about 24 ms plain, about 30 ms with those of 33). Found on the way: drawing the in-between picture into the state the next frame continues from made the replays drift (the motion blur's copies took it in twice: artifacts the user saw); a host-side save and restore was exact but cost 9 ms a frame, hence the GPU copies. Left: things that appear in a frame (a hit flash, smoke) show one picture early; the user's look in play.
   **Stability, 2026-10-21 (the user: "not stable and causes slight artifacts"; Tenkaichi3Decomp's ways checked):** the bone matrices (34-quadword unpacks, quadwords 22 to 24 and 26 to 28) and the camera's axes (12-quadword unpacks, quadwords 0 to 2 and 4 to 6) are blended as turning, each axis keeping the blended length of its two originals (a plain average shrinks them: the fighters' "2D" look Tenkaichi3Decomp's players reported); no in-between picture across a camera cut (a view axis turning by more than 25 degrees between the two frames; `cameraTurn`); and the in-between picture is never waited for: one not ready at its field is left out. Measured (window, stage 8 versus fight, 1,500 flips): plain 30 flips and about 60 pictures per second, 1,269 in-between pictures shown, 188 camera cuts, 22 not ready; with HD textures the game's thread needs 33 to 40 ms a flip at 4x to 16x (27 to 22 flips per second): HD textures and 60 fps together do not fit on this PC yet (each alone does). Left: the cost of HD textures with 60 fps (both renderers name textures and the HD lookups), the camera-cut threshold (188 cuts in 1,500 flips may be too many), the user's look.
4. **Performance — measured 2026-10-21** (above): on a worker thread; the second renderer replaced by a state copy.
   **2026-10-25: the crash and the threads.** Unpaced (headless) runs with 60 fps crashed with heap corruption within 9 to 20 s, with and without HD textures: AddressSanitizer and a lock check found Granite dropping a used staging block larger than its pool's size without keeping it until its frame ends, so paraLLEl-GS's pending copies held the last reference and freed the buffer and its memory without the device lock, on the second renderer's thread while the main one used the same lists (`tools/graphics/gpu/granite_retired_blocks.patch`; with it, 2,500 flips under ASan with no unlocked free). Each renderer now has a GS thread of its own (`gs/gpu/gs_render_thread.*`, [performance plan](../ee/PERFORMANCE_PLAN.md) item 3): the in-between pictures' thread runs the blended VIF1 lists and checks the GS data while the second renderer's GS thread names textures and draws. Checked: the game state with 60 fps on equals the run without it on all 6,534 fields (state hash), and the main picture is pixel-equal with it on. Measured in the window (CPU vs CPU, Release, 16x): without HD a fight holds 30 flips and shows 2,067 of 2,181 in-between pictures (7.6 ms each); with HD the fight holds 30 flips but an in-between picture takes 15 to 18 ms in that battle (7.5 ms in the gate's fight), longer than the field it has, so almost none is shown: the second renderer's naming of textures is most of it (next: it takes the main renderer's names).
4b. **Flicker and glitches fixed — 2026-10-25** (seen by the user): the blend mixed unrelated data (the game's V4-32 data is streamed in chunks that shift between frames; objects carry copies of the camera's screen matrices), the in-between picture came 9.7 ms after the main one, and the fighters' HD variant could differ. Now rigid transforms and cameras only, with every camera copy blended alike; shown at the next field's deadline while the in-between pictures keep up; drawn palettes reach the second renderer's names one flip late ([vif1_blend.h](../../../runtime/vu/vif1_blend.h), October history). Left: transforms cut in two at chunk edges stay the newer frame's, the user's look.
4c. **HD and 60 fps together — 2026-10-26.** A fight frame's VIF1 list (one transfer of about 600,000 words) comes right after the flip before it, about 33 ms before its own flip, and nothing follows it. The in-between picture of the last two frames is therefore drawn as the newer frame comes: each VIF1 transfer goes, with the input before it, to the in-between thread, which blends and runs it while the game goes on; at the flip only the check that the lists paired and the scanout are left (`InBetweenFrames::hand`, `drawEvents`). Each list is walked once (kept for its turn as the older frame) and the blend's quadword compares are inline (blend 2.65 ms to about 1.3). Making it hold with HD needed the device's frame changes untangled ([performance plan](../ee/PERFORMANCE_PLAN.md) item 3): a frame change (Granite's next frame context) waits for every open command buffer and, under the device's lock, for older GPU work, so (a) the main renderer's paraLLEl-GS flushes no longer change the frame (only our flip, a readback and a picture shown do), (b) the GPU work a frame change waits for is waited for before it, outside the lock (`awaitFrameChange`), (c) the GS threads stand still with their work submitted while the frame changes (`GsRenderThread::whilePaused`) instead of being drained, and (d) pictures are presented on a thread of their own: in a window the driver's present waited about 4 ms for the compositor with 60 pictures a second, holding the device's lock (Granite patch `granite_wsi_end_frame_context.patch`: the frame context ends while the threads stand still, the present after). Measured in the window (CPU vs CPU, Release, 16x, widescreen): with HD the fight holds 29.97 flips per second throughout and 1,941 of 2,335 in-between pictures are shown (before: 30 flips with about a third shown, then 23.6 to 28 flips per second at the first streamed version); without HD 2,079 of 2,335 at 29.96 to 29.98. The GPU is not the limit (the main renderer's picture 9.0 ms with and without HD; the wait before frame changes 0.01 ms). Checked: check mode and replay mode exact with and without HD (5 flips each; replay with HD differs by under 100 bytes at flips 400 and 401 also before this change). Left: an HD in-between picture takes about 18 ms of the in-between thread and the game's thread waits about 4 ms a flip for it at flips (within the frame's slack on this PC); the user's look.
4d. **Slower processors — 2026-10-26** ([performance plan](../ee/PERFORMANCE_PLAN.md) item 4c): the in-between threads run below normal priority; the in-between thread may run up to a frame behind the game (finished frames queue for it; the main renderer keeps three state slots), and a picture finished after its field is left out, so the game's thread no longer waits for it (8.3 s in a fight on 4 cores with 8 threads before, 0.4 ms after); when the game would still wait at more than a quarter of the last 64 flips, 60 fps turns itself off for the run. Check mode with HD still exact. Also fixed: a second renderer's names stayed in the main renderer's palette list after it was stopped.
4e. **A flat 60 in fights, and the fighters' blend faults — 2026-10-26** (the user: dips during combat; then a fighter's body texture flickering where it should not be in fast moves). (a) Delayed fights: in a fight every picture is shown a field later, the flip's own held for the next field (`GpuGsBackend::holdPicture`, `showHeld`) and the in-between picture of the job started at the flip before shown at the flip's deadline, each job's picture held in its own slot; an in-between picture has a field more (16.7 ms more input delay in fights only; `BT2_SIXTY_FPS_DELAY=0` the old timing). In the window at 16x with HD, widescreen and the 4x scanout: 3,472 of 3,545 shown (2,999 before), one not ready in time; the pictures' order checked by a log (one picture a field, main and in-between in turn). (b) With nearly every in-between picture shown, its faults were seen: found by scoring 60 in-between pictures of a CPU fight against the two main pictures around them (pixels the two agree on but the in-between picture does not). Three kinds: a piece of a fighter put elsewhere (a transform paired with another bone through the shift search for moved data), the skin torn in fast moves (bones turning past the 45-degree limit left at the newer pose beside blended ones), and a camera cut blended as two far poses. Now: bones may turn up to 150 degrees, and moved data is not searched for (it stays the newer frame's); the score fell from 68,105 to 52,295 misplaced pixels, and what remains above the noise is outlines that really move. (c) The scanout's 4x choice is now made at every flip for both renderers alike (it was skipped at flips without a picture headless). Left: the game's own frames in heavy moments (flip 3360 of the CPU fight: 27.8 frames per second, the game's thread waiting for the main GS thread, which spends about 9% of its time getting command buffers under the device's lock the second renderer also takes, and about 15% naming textures); camera cuts that turn by less than 25 degrees but move far; the user's look.
5. **The toggle — built 2026-10-21:** "60 fps battles" on the Display page of the launcher and the in-game menu, off by default, from the next start. The check that the fight is unchanged with it on was done 2026-10-25 (state hash, above). Left: the user's look.
