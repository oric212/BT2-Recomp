# Image quality and widescreen

Requested by the user on 2026-10-20 with the [HD textures](HD_TEXTURES_PLAN.md) and [60 fps](HIGH_FRAME_RATE_PLAN.md) plans: the best picture the port can give, and widescreen.

## Where detail comes from

Three separate steps decide how sharp the picture is:

1. **Rendering resolution:** how many pixels the GS work is computed at. The game draws at 640 × 448. This is the only step that adds detail; HD textures pay off only as far as it goes.
2. **Texture detail:** the textures sampled while rendering ([HD textures](HD_TEXTURES_PLAN.md)).
3. **Display scaling:** how the finished picture is enlarged to the window. Integer scaling (whole multiples, each pixel a sharp block) or smooth scaling (bilinear, or FSR1 with sharpening as paraLLEl-GS's author suggests) changes how the pixels look, but adds no detail. Integer scaling is already a display setting ([launcher plan](../launcher/LAUNCHER_PLAN.md), Overlay).

**What paraLLEl-GS gives.** Super-sampling at 2×, 4×, 8× or 16×, and with 4× or more a scanout at twice the resolution (1,280 × 896) built from the samples. 16× is 4 × 4 samples per game pixel. The scanout at 2× uses them as 2 × 2 output pixels with 2 × 2 samples each (anti-aliased). A scanout at 4× (2,560 × 1,792, one sample per pixel, no anti-aliasing) is the most detail the samples hold; paraLLEl-GS does not offer it, and the port adds it (step 3). Its notes warn that a game which copies its frame to the display buffer through a texture loses the extra detail; BT2 has to be checked for that.

**Integer scaling with these:** 1,280 × 896 fits a 1080p screen once (with borders) and a 4K screen twice (2,560 × 1,792). Widescreen stretches the picture horizontally by 4/3, which is not a whole multiple: there, integer scaling can apply vertically only, or smooth scaling is used.

## Widescreen

PCSX2's patch database has a 16:9 patch for BT2's PAL release (SLES-54164, by sergx12, ported to PAL by ElHecht), none for SLUS-21441. It changes the projection: four floats of 432.0 (`0x43D88000`) to about 576 (cut-scenes, gameplay, versus 1P and 2P), the instructions that load 0.75 or 1.0 next to them, and one for the sky. Our disc has the same kind of table: 432.0 at `0x386D94` to `0x386E2C`, and `lui at, 0x3F40` near `0x12BA08` (the PAL sky fix is at `0x12BD38`). So the patch's behaviour can be found in our executable and applied as a setting, with the authors credited. The window then shows the picture at 16:9. Open questions: whether the HUD and menus stretch (the patch may leave 2D at 4:3 positions), and whether objects are culled at the new screen edges.

**Built 2026-10-20.** PCSX2's database also has a patch for our release (`patches/SLUS-21441_FE961D28.pnach`, Widescreen 16:9, commit `9390265`; its CRC equals our executable's): six floats (the projections' width 433 to about 577 at `0x386DA0`, `0x386DD8`, `0x386DF4`, `0x386E00`, and the horizontal scale 7/6 to 14/9 at `0x386DA4`, `0x386DC8`) and two `lui` loading float constants (0.75 to 0.5625 at `0x12BA08`, 1.0 to 0.75 at `0x25B694`). The port adds `0x386D94`, a fifth projection width of 433 that the patch leaves alone: it is the story map's (with the patch alone the map is stretched; with it the map shows wider at the right proportions; the fight's pictures do not change). How it is applied: `runtime/ee/game_patches.cpp` holds the words and writes them after the executable is loaded, checking each original word first; the two instructions are read from guest memory by the generated code (`prepare_runtime.py` rewrites the `lui` at the addresses `config/game_patches.json` lists), so a patch is a memory write for compiled and interpreted code alike. `BT2_GAME_PATCHES=widescreen` in tool runs; the launcher's and the overlay's Display entry "Widescreen" (from the next start). The window then shows the picture 16:9 (its width the height × 16/9; integer scaling counts whole multiples vertically). Checked: a versus fight, the Ultimate Battle Z screens and the story map (`work/frames/widescreen/`): the 3D scenes show a wider view with unchanged proportions; 2D screens and the HUD stretch, as in PCSX2 with the patch; nothing interpreted; the fight's work per picture unchanged (19.5 ms). The window copies pixels for the stretch (uneven columns); GDI's filtered stretch cost about 12 ms per picture, so filtered scaling waits for presentation on the GPU (performance plan, presenting without readback). Left: the user's look in play; 2D screens at 4:3 inside the 16:9 picture (pillarboxed) would need the game's 2D drawing told apart from 3D and is not planned yet.

## Order of work

1. **Widescreen — built 2026-10-20** (above): the SLUS-21441 patch plus the story map, a setting, the window at 16:9; pictures for the user.
2. **Super-sampling and high-resolution output** as settings (with [HD textures](HD_TEXTURES_PLAN.md) step 4), measured in Release. **Switch built 2026-10-20:** `BT2_SUPER_SAMPLING=<1|2|4|8|16>`; 4 or more scan out at 1,280 × 896. The Ultimate Battle Z screens and a fight with pack 2 (2,000 flips, Release): 27 s at 4×, 31 s at 16×, no renderer errors; the picture at 1,280 × 896 with smooth edges, the select screen's portraits crisp at twice the size (`work/frames/hd-textures/zoom-*.png`: original, HD at 1×, HD at 16×). Left: the setting for players and the frame time in a fight at each rate.
3. **A 4× scanout — built 2026-10-26** (the user compared the main menu with HD textures against a PCSX2 picture: ours blurry). Two causes, both found by measuring how much detail a picture holds inside each game pixel: the scanout stopped at 2× (1,280 × 896), so a 4K window stretched it; and paraLLEl-GS draws 2D art (sprites, and flat triangles without perspective, which BT2's menu art is) with one texture coordinate per pixel, so an HD replacement never showed more than the original's detail. `tools/graphics/gpu/parallel_gs_quad_scanout.patch`: with 16 samples on the ordered grid the scanout can be 4× (2,560 × 1,792, one sample per pixel); the probe picks it when the window shows the picture taller than 2× (`BT2_SCANOUT_QUAD=1` forces it without a window); runs without a window stay at 2×, so the gate's pictures are unchanged. `tools/graphics/gpu/parallel_gs_replacement_detail.patch`: a 2D primitive drawn with a replacement takes its texture coordinates per sample (triangle setup centres them as paraLLEl-GS's super-sampled textures; binning draws its tiles super-sampled), held inside the primitive's own texels (a texture entry of its own, region-clamped at the middles of its edge texels, as the BT3 port's notes describe), and a magnified one is filtered linearly. Both patches carry their changed shaders as SPIR-V made with the Vulkan SDK's glslc (the flags reproduce the bank's own words). Checked: the main menu, Dueling and character select at 4× beside the PCSX2 picture (`work/frames/menus/hd4x-090725-*.png`), no slivers at the pieces' edges; a fight's HUD sharp; 3D unchanged (the fighters' outlines stay the game's full-screen pass at its own resolution). Not yet seen by the user.
4. **Effects at the super-sampled resolution — built 2026-10-26** (the user: in a paused super attack the scene behind the pause box was in game-pixel blocks, with an odd line of coloured garbage at the bottom of the screen). Found with a dump of a flip's GIF data (`BT2_GIF_DUMP`, `tools/graphics/gpu/gif_dump_draws.py`) and pictures of the game's buffers between its draws (`BT2_GS_PEEK`). A fight's frame ends in a chain of full-screen passes much like BT3's: the depth's middle byte into the frame's alpha through a 16-bit view (the depth-of-field weight), the scene downscaled 2:1 and 4:1 and blended back by that weight, and the ink outline (below); the pause blends a quarter-size copy of the scene over the whole frame. paraLLEl-GS read those buffers from their one-sample copies, so at 4x each game pixel of the blur, and of the blend's edge around the fighters, was a block. `tools/graphics/gpu/parallel_gs_super_sampled_effects.patch` and the runtime:
   - **Super-sampled textures** (paraLLEl-GS's own option, on with any super-sampling; `BT2_SUPER_SAMPLED_TEXTURES=0` turns it off): the passes read the buffers with their samples, so the depth of field, the blend's edges and the pause's background keep the resolution. The texture replacements' 2D primitives carry a bit of their own and keep step 3's per-sample path, which the option's rules for the game's buffers would otherwise take over (the HUD went blocky).
   - **The outline as the game draws it:** the ink compares each pixel's material id with its neighbour one pixel to the right (a 16-bit buffer), then widens the result upward by copies 0, 1 and 2 pixels away. Per sample, the comparison gives a near-horizontal edge a band as thin as its slope, and the three copies then show as three thin parallel lines. A pass into a 16-bit buffer that reads super-sampled texels 1 to 4 whole pixels away from a 1:1 copy is now drawn single-sampled, so the outline is the game's own band again, at its resolution (step 5).
   - **The bottom line:** two of the passes read past what was drawn: the 2:1 blend's last line samples a row of the half-size buffer the downscale did not write, and the pause's quarter-size picture (128 × 128) is clamped at 255, so its last three lines blend in the memory after it. The GS does the same; a TV's overscan hid it. Super-sampled, a region clamp now stops at the texture's own size (as PCSX2's hardware renderer's copies of textures do), and the high-resolution scanout shows the line above in place of the circuit's last line (`sample_circuit.frag`; the 1x picture stays the original's).
   - Checked: pictures with the option on and off over the start (logos, films, title), the first story map, the gate's fight, the Dueling screen and a fight with pack 2 (start and menus identical; HUD as sharp as before; fights and story map smoother behind the characters); the pause picture's bottom lines and a fight's last line; 60 fps check mode exact at 16x and 4x; GPU time in a fight at 16x 8.2 ms against 7.4 ms. Pictures for the user: `work/frames/effects-141212-*.png`.
5. **HD outlines (next):** the ink band is computed per game pixel, so at 4x it steps in blocks of 4 x 4. The game's own passes cannot give a smooth band at a higher resolution (step 4). The way, as in the BT3 port (`work/references/bt3r/docs/NATIVE-RENDER-SEAM.md`, its `outline.frag`): recognise the ink chain's passes in paraLLEl-GS (the same 1:1 neighbour reads into a 16-bit buffer) and compute the band on the GPU per sample instead: a pixel is ink where the material id under the palette changes within one game pixel to the right (and the game's widening of two pixels upward), measured in samples, so the band keeps the original's thickness with smooth edges. Checked against the game's own band at 1x (they must match exactly there) and by close-ups at 4x for the user.
6. **Display scaling:** integer (both axes for 4:3, vertical only for 16:9), bilinear, FSR1 with sharpening; the user compares pictures. Newer upscalers (FSR 2 to 4, DLSS, XeSS) are temporal: they need per-pixel motion vectors and depth from the renderer and blend detail across frames; the PS2's drawing gives neither and faked vectors ghost, while super-sampling already renders the real detail. So the choice is among spatial upscalers on the finished picture: FSR 1 (EASU with RCAS sharpening, open source) first, with a sharpness setting; NVIDIA Image Scaling or Snapdragon GSR as alternatives for the user to compare (the user asked about something newer than FSR 1, 2026-10-20). The picture is shown on the GPU since 2026-10-20 (a stretch that is not a whole multiple is filtered).
7. **Post-process anti-aliasing** (the user, 2026-10-20: "anti aliasing of some kind"): super-sampling is anti-aliasing at its most thorough, but it leaves edges the game draws itself without samples (the fighters' outlines, drawn as offset copies, and 2D screens) and costs the graphics card in proportion to the samples. An edge filter on the finished picture (FXAA or SMAA, a compute pass in the window presenter) costs well under a millisecond and smooths those too; a Display setting next to super-sampling.
